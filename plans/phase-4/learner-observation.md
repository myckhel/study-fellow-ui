## Session setup (facilitator)

Use 2–3 secondary-school biology learners who have encountered photosynthesis and can describe a relevant confusion; vary prior familiarity where practical. Do not describe this small convenience sample as representative. Before sessions, arrange appropriate learner/guardian or school permission where needed, confirm voluntary participation, and explain that stopping has no consequence. Qualified content review remains pending: do not present the session as verified biology instruction.

Allow 15–20 minutes each on the learner's available device, or desktop Chrome if that is the only available surface. Record actual device/browser and setting. A desktop session cannot close the physical-phone/software-keyboard gap. Use anonymized IDs `L01`, `L02`, `L03`; do not put names, contact details, school identifiers, credentials, or raw recordings in this repository. Obtain separate agreement before recording; written observation is sufficient. Ask learners to avoid personal information in their input. Use a fresh app session per learner through the supported confirmed restart, without deleting another person's useful session.

Opening script: “We are checking how this tool works for you. We are testing the tool, not you. Some answers are checked by limited rules, so it may misunderstand your words. Please use your own words and tell me what you are looking for. You can stop or skip anything.”

Do not show the prepared path or correct answer before the learner's first attempt. Do not choose a misconception for them or ask them to reproduce supplied examples. If they need help, record the exact prompt and what happened first. Help after an unaided attempt is allowed but marks that task as assisted.

## Tasks (give one at a time)

| Task | Neutral instruction / follow-up | Observe before interpreting |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Unaided entry | “Use this to get help with something you find unclear about how plants use light to make sugar.” After entry: “What would you expect to happen next?” | Input in their own words, unsupported-input handling, sample use, diagnostic choice or skip, hesitation, requested help. |
| 2. Route comprehension | “Tell me what you think you will do here, then begin when you are ready.” | Their paraphrase of the outcome/route; whether accepting or correcting it is understood; action used. Do not explain the route first. |
| 3. Teaching and visual | “Use this part as you normally would. Tell me when you are ready to continue.” Afterwards: “What did that picture or text mean to you?” | Reading/scrolling, diagram/text-equivalent use, source discovery, matter/energy confusion, Continue discovery. Do not explain the diagram for them. |
| 4. Clarification | “If there is something you want explained, ask it here. If nothing is unclear, tell me how you would ask for help.” | Actual question and reply, whether place/draft remain, difference between Send and Check answer, ability to return to answer. Do not supply the supported clarification phrase. |
| 5. Guided response and adaptation | “Answer this in your own words, and continue as you normally would.” After feedback: “What do you think happened? What will you do next?” | Exact answer, classification, visible route change/skip, learner's interpretation, assistance. If no adaptive branch arises naturally, mark adaptation **not observed**. |
| 6. Fresh check and feedback | “Try this new situation in your own words.” After feedback: “What does this tell you about your answer?” | Original transfer answer, separate prediction/reasoning evidence, uncertainty, retry response/result if offered, whether first/retry or examples are confused. Do not coach before submission. |
| 7. Leave and resume | Before completion where possible: “Imagine you have to stop now. Leave this page and come back after reloading. Continue where you think you left off.” | Save indicator comprehension, actual resumed step, draft/answer preservation, recovery action, assistance and data-loss signs. Do not claim saved until the app confirms it. |
| 8. Close | “What was useful? What was difficult? What would you expect to happen if you came back tomorrow?” | Exact comments, expectation of saved progress and available topics, confidence unsupported by evidence, one proposed improvement. |

Neutral rescue prompts: “What are you looking for?”, “What do you expect that to do?”, “Please tell me what you are thinking.” Allow roughly 30–60 seconds of stuck behavior before offering navigation help; stop sooner if the learner wants to stop. Never say “click Continue”, “light is energy”, or “Plant A” during an unaided attempt. Log any such assistance if given.

If the natural path does not expose adaptation, optionally offer a **separate assisted walkthrough after all unaided tasks**, clearly recorded as such. A supplied response or facilitator-chosen misconception is interface feedback, not the learner's reasoning and not an outcome improvement.
